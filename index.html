I build large-scale physics simulations and turn the data they produce into answers.

For my M.Sc. at McGill University, in Professor Nikolas Provatas’s group, I modelled how Aluminium–Silicon alloys form their microstructure during Laser Powder Bed Fusion, the process behind metal 3D printing. I extended the open-source OpenPFC framework from pure materials to binary alloys in C++/MPI, ran 3D simulations on grids up to 2048 × 1024 × 1024 on Canada’s national supercomputers, and wrote the pipeline that turned 100 TB+ of output into precipitate and dislocation statistics. The project was a Mitacs Accelerate collaboration with Hydro-Québec and experimental teams at the University of New Brunswick and McMaster University.

The same skills carry straight into industry: fast numerical code, analysis pipelines that hold up, and the habit of checking whether a result is physically and statistically believable before trusting it.
